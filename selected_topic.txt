Freudian development and CEN

How Freud's stage model (oral, anal, phallic, latency, genital) maps onto cases of CEN; where early relational absence causes fixation or arrested development; the significance of the "quiet infant who didn't cry" as a potential marker of very early learned helplessness or shutdown — emotional suppression that begins before language; what this early onset means for character structure and adult presentation

---
ANGLE & HOOK PLAN

Hook: Open with the Still Face Experiment — a parent goes blank for 90 seconds and a baby cycles through confusion, protest, and collapse. Then pivot: what if the still face wasn't an experiment? What if it was just Tuesday? This frames the whole piece — CEN isn't a single traumatic event but the chronic version of that experiment, running for years.

Angle: Freud built a theory for too much — seduction, violence, overstimulation. CEN is the wound of too little, and that asymmetry is where the piece lives. The oral stage (0–18 months) is ground zero: both deprivation AND over-indulgence produce fixation but in different flavours — the oral-deprivation adult is the cynic who expects disappointment, Karl Abraham's "oral rage" made into a character structure. The "quiet good baby" is the central dark surprise: silence isn't self-regulation, it's the infant equivalent of giving up — learned helplessness forming before language, before the capacity for explicit memory. Winnicott's False Self maps onto CEN with uncanny precision: compliance becomes the template for adult identity when emotional mirroring was absent, producing adults who feel they are performing a life rather than living one. Close on alexithymia as the adult echo — the inability to name emotions not because they were repressed, but because they were never named by anyone else first; the internal vocabulary was simply never built.
