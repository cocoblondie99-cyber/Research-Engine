AI qualifications and certifications

officially recognised courses and credentials in AI and machine learning; vendor certifications (Microsoft AI-900, Azure AI Engineer, Google Cloud, AWS); academic and professional body qualifications; what carries genuine weight with employers vs what is marketing; where to start depending on technical background

ANGLE/HOOK PLAN:
Hook on the central paradox: in the fastest-growing job market in tech, the credential designed to signal competence is increasingly not what employers are actually hiring for — portfolios and deployed projects now carry more weight than the certificate alone. The piece should map the landscape from the ground up: the free-or-not-worth-it fundamentals tier (AI-900, Google AI Essentials — useful for the vocabulary, not the visa); the platform-tied vendor certs (AWS ML Specialty, Google Professional ML Engineer, Azure AI Engineer — only valuable if you're already in that cloud ecosystem, with serious prep time required); the platform-agnostic learning tier (DeepLearning.AI / Andrew Ng, fast.ai — unusually respected because the instructor's name carries genuine researcher credibility); and the degree question (master's still preferred for senior/research roles; 65% of advanced ML postings list it). The surprising angle to linger on: only 23% of AI certificate programmes carry recognised accreditation vs 89% of degree programmes — the market is flooded with certificates of wildly uneven quality, and "AI certified" as a phrase has already become almost meaningless. End with a practical map for three reader types: the non-technical professional wanting to follow the conversation (AI-900/Google AI Essentials), the career-changer with some technical background (DeepLearning.AI specialisation + build three portfolio projects), the practitioner wanting to signal cloud expertise (pick the cert that matches your organisation's platform). The closing thought: a certification is a vocabulary test; what employers are actually buying is proof you've spoken the language in the real world.
