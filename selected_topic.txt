Emotional literacy and interoception (CEN)
in the context of CEN recovery: the skill of reading your own bodily emotional signals and why it is specifically underdeveloped in people who grew up in emotionally neglectful environments; how the absence of co-regulation in childhood leaves the interoceptive map incomplete; what it means to learn to feel emotions as physical sensations as an adult; practical approaches to developing this capacity and why it matters for recovery

ANGLE AND HOOK PLAN:
Hook: open with the heartbeat detection exercise — invite the reader to count their heartbeats for thirty seconds without touching their pulse, then reveal that accuracy here is a direct window into interoceptive ability. This makes the abstract immediately visceral and personal.

Core arc: the piece moves through four ideas in sequence. First, the surprising neuroscience: emotions aren't pre-formed things that arrive from somewhere — the brain constructs them by interpreting raw interoceptive signals in context (Lisa Feldman Barrett's theory of constructed emotion). Second, how co-regulation in childhood is the mechanism that builds the interpretive layer: caregivers don't just soothe — they name, mirror, and contextualise bodily sensations, teaching the child what this flutter in the chest means, what this tightness in the throat is called. Third, what CEN specifically breaks: not the body's signals (those keep firing), but the translation layer — the child grows up with a body that speaks and a mind that cannot read it, which maps to the clinical concept of alexithymia (difficulty identifying feelings, common in CEN: emotional neglect strongly predicts both difficulty identifying feelings and difficulty distinguishing them from bodily sensations). Fourth, what developing this in adulthood actually looks like — somatic practices (body scan, heartbeat tracking, MABT), affect labelling (naming an emotion reduces amygdala activation by up to 50%), and the slow work of building emotional granularity — learning not just "bad" but the finer distinctions between grief, shame, disappointment, dread.

Surprising angle to linger on: for CEN adults, the problem isn't that emotions don't happen — the body always fires. The problem is that the signal never gets opened. Recovery is retrofitting the translation layer that was never installed. This reframe matters: it shifts the story from "something is wrong with you" to "something was never taught to you," and it makes the path forward concrete and learnable.
