CEN and autism overlap

The relationship between childhood emotional neglect and autism spectrum presentations: where the two genuinely correlate (alexithymia, social withdrawal, sensory sensitivity, masking); where CEN can false-flag as autism (acquired emotional blunting mistaken for innate social deficit); where autism increases CEN risk (atypical children more likely to receive misattuned caregiving); what the research actually says about co-occurrence rates; how to distinguish acquired vs innate presentation in adults; what the distinction means for treatment approach

--- ANGLE PLAN ---
Hook: alexithymia — the inability to identify or name your own emotions — is the symptom at the crossroads. It appears in roughly 50% of autistic people AND is strongly linked to CEN via secondary (environmentally acquired) emotional blunting; the same fog can arrive by two entirely different roads, and getting the road wrong derails treatment. Structure the piece around three directions of overlap: (1) CEN mimicking autism — severe neglect can produce quasi-autistic social and emotional impairments, and RAD is a documented misdiagnosis risk; (2) autism generating the conditions for CEN — autistic children face maltreatment at 3× the rate of neurotypical peers, neurotypical parents frequently misattune to atypical children via the double empathy problem (bidirectional misunderstanding that starts in the family home), and 44% of autistic adults have PTSD vs ~8% in the general adolescent population; (3) what distinguishes innate from acquired — restricted/repetitive behaviours and sensory sensitivities are specific to ASD and not produced by neglect alone, and secondary alexithymia (neglect-rooted) responds to therapy in ways primary alexithymia (neurological) may not, making the distinction clinically high-stakes. Close on the idea that the distinction is not about blame — a neurotypical parent can misattune to an autistic child with no malice whatsoever — but about finding the right map for repair.
