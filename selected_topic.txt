The HPA axis and chronic stress (CEN)
in the context of CEN recovery: how childhood emotional neglect dysregulates the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal stress system; the difference between acute and chronic low-level HPA activation; what years of low-grade hypervigilance do to sleep, immunity, memory, metabolism, and emotional regulation; why the neglect-affected nervous system reads ordinary situations as threatening; what HPA repair looks like in practice

ANGLE AND HOOK PLAN:
Hook on a specific, strange fact: scientists can measure the effects of emotional absence in a saliva sample. The cortisol awakening response — the sharp spike in cortisol that should arrive thirty minutes after waking — is flattened or missing in people with a history of early neglect, a biological fingerprint left by years of no one coming. The key angle is the counterintuitive twist most people get wrong: CEN doesn't simply leave the stress system switched on. It can flip the system in the opposite direction — the HPA axis eventually downregulates into hypocortisolism, producing not anxiety but flatness, exhaustion, numbness, and a blunted capacity to mobilise under pressure. Use the metaphor of a smoke detector recalibrated to a more dangerous environment: in childhood it was set exquisitely sensitive (neuroception attuned to tone of voice, quality of silence, parental emotional temperature); in adulthood that same detector keeps firing — or, after decades, burns out its battery. The repair section should bring in somatic, relational, and sleep-based approaches as ways of *retuning* the detector, connecting to the earned-secure-attachment and somatic-repair threads already in this series.
